3

Get the $500 onto the chain

you · 20 min

There is no way to deposit directly. Money has to be bridged in from another chain. Three moves:

  1. Buy $500 of ETH on whatever exchange you normally use.
  2. Withdraw it to your new MetaMask address — choose the Arbitrum or Base network on the withdrawal screen if offered, because withdrawing on Ethereum mainnet can cost $5–20 in fees on its own.
  3. Bridge it across at relay.link/bridge/robinhood — pick Robinhood Chain as the destination. Robinhood's own documentation lists Relay as one of two bridges that settle in seconds.
Send a small test first. Bridge $20, check it lands, then bridge the rest. A wrong address or wrong network on the first try costs you the whole $500, and there is nobody to call.
Gas is ETH here, and it is absurdly cheap — the network was charging 0.028 gwei when I checked, which works out at about 2 cents a swap. You do not need to think about gas again after this step.

7

When an alert fires, buy

you · 3 min per trade
  1. The alert gives you a token address. Copy it.
  2. Open app.uniswap.org, network Robinhood Chain, sell USDG, paste the token address in the buy box.
  3. Amount: $75. Always $75. Do not size up because one feels good.
  4. Set slippage to 3%. If the screen warns the price impact is above 3%, skip the trade — that coin is too thin and it is the one rule that has saved this model money.
  5. Swap. It confirms in a second or two.
Good news you can actually rely on: being slow barely costs you. We measured the price 30 s, 60 s, 3 min, 5 min and 10 min after each of 48 real wallet buys. The typical extra cost of arriving ten minutes late is under 1% — and it is no worse at 10 minutes than at 30 seconds. You do not have to race anyone.
The catch: that is the typical case. The spread widens a lot the longer you wait — at ten minutes, one trade in ten was 17% cheaper and one in ten was 15% dearer. So inside an hour is fine; skip it entirely if you are half a day late.
If you are asleep, you are asleep. Signals land at every hour of the day — there is no quiet window. Just skip the ones you miss and write down that you skipped them.
8

Selling — three rules, no judgement

you

Whichever of these happens first:

  • The wallet sells. You sell too, the same day you see it. This is how 115 of 266 paper positions ended.
  • You are up 3×. Sell the lot. That is the rule the paper book runs on, and 12 of its trades ended this way.
  • Four days pass. Sell regardless. 142 positions hit this once the rule actually worked — the wallet simply never sold, and the money was better used elsewhere.
Found on 18 Aug: the simulator does not actually obey this rule, and you will. Its 4-day exit only fires when another tracked wallet happens to trade that same coin again — so on a coin that goes quiet, which is exactly the coin you want out of, the timer can never fire at all. Right now 56 of the 62 open paper positions are past their exit, the oldest by 18 days, holding $53,500 of stake at −$21,097. That is a bug in the paper book, not in this plan — you will sell on day 4 by hand, which is what the rule was always supposed to do.
The one honestly untested part of this whole plan. We measured how much a late buy costs. The same measurement for selling never ran — the file has zero sell samples. So how much a late exit costs you is unknown. Treat the sell alert as the urgent one and the buy alert as the relaxed one, which is the opposite of what feels natural.

Know how it ends before it starts

you

Write these two lines down now, while nothing is at stake:

  • Stop at 15 completed trades, whatever the P&L. Then we compare your 15 real fills against the same 15 in the paper book.
  • Or stop if the $500 becomes $300. Not "review" — stop.
What a normal run looks like: about 2 signals a day, so 15 trades takes roughly a week and a half. Expect a flat or red first week even if this works perfectly — only 1 trade in 9 doubles, and the median trade is a small loss. The profit lives entirely in the rare big winners, so a stretch of small losses is the normal texture of it, not a failure.
The single rule that matters most: when the $500 is gone, it is gone. No top-up, not once. The whole value of this test is that it has a fixed, known, survivable cost.

What this test can and cannot prove

It can prove: that we can see a buy, act on it, get a fill near the modelled price, and get out again — with real money and a real human in the loop. It also produces the first real number for something the model currently guesses at: the gap between the modelled fill and yours.

It cannot prove there is an edge, and it is worth being blunt about why. With 6 slots and a couple of signals a day, you will take some signals and skip others, and which you skip depends on when you were awake — that is not a sample anyone can draw a conclusion from. The paper book itself is now negative: −$2,975 net on 291 positions, the control-arm test came back p = 0.076 on 8 positions versus 44, and the median trade loses money. Right shape, never significant — the same shape arbforge died in.

And one caveat on the book itself, found today: because dead positions cannot exit, losses pile up in the open column while winners leave through the door marked "sold at 3×". That is why the realised number always looks better than the net one. Fixing the exit moved the paper number down, exactly as expected — just much further than expected: +$23,387 to −$2,975.

The real proof still to come is the control arm re-run on the full 107-trade book, which is a computer job, costs nothing, and I can start whenever you say. Your $500 answers a different and smaller question, and it is worth answering — just not the big one.

So how long should we hold? Here is the answer.

The equity table above confounds two things, so I separated them. For every position that was still alive at age A — the only ones a time limit can touch — I compared exiting then against what actually happened. That isolates the exit decision from the fact that freeing cash funds more trades.

Two columns kill the question. The median is exactly $0 at every single horizon — for the typical position, holding longer changes nothing at all, because the coin simply stops trading. And the entire mean benefit is four trades out of 144 (UP, BRODIE, POOLS, YOLO): remove them and holding longer is negative at every horizon tested. There is no holding period in this data. The 22-point spread between "4 days" and "never" is those four trades and nothing else.
And the reason that question stopped mattering. I re-ran the placebo test on the fixed book — 2,757 events, 1,473 copied buys. Copying these wallets against darts thrown at coins of the same depth, same size, same hold, same moment: +3.93 percentage points, p = 0.313. The most flattering framing available gives z = 1.695. arbforge died at z = 1.69. The harness's own verdict: 3 FAIL, 5 UNDERPOWERED, 0 PASS — no arm is a result, and arm A's whole profit is its single best trade (strip it: +$860 → −$483).
My answer: leave it at 4 days. Not because 4 days is right — on this sample it is among the worst settings — but because there is no edge for a holding rule to protect, and moving it to "never" to collect +19% would be fitting the dial to four trades. Four days keeps capital cycling, which gathers samples faster, which is the only thing that can actually settle this.
And here is why I have not just set it to "never". Picking the horizon that back-tests best, on one month of data, where four trades carry the entire result, is precisely the overfitting that killed arbforge. The monotonic shape is a real clue, but "hold forever" also parks the money: at that setting 62 positions stay open and $58,000 sits tied up, which is the exact problem the 4-day rule was introduced to solve on 6 August. That trade-off is real, but it is now moot: see the answer above.
What I changed and what I did not. I fixed the rule so it fires on a clock instead of waiting for someone to trade the coin, and I gave the alert bot the same burst guard the open side already had, so a mass restatement sends one message instead of 142. I did not touch your production settings — live_config.json still says 96 hours. Changing that is one line and one word from you.
